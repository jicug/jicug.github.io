- Presenter: Seigo Tanaka, a freelance engineer (business name: 1ft Seabass).

- He didn't let Bob commit automatically — keeping that under human control let things move along smoothly.

### Theme 2: Developing the Izu Stamp Rally (Ichiro Kano)

- Presenter: Ichiro Kano, an IBM Champion who also works as a programming instructor, among other roles.
- App built: Izu Stamp Rally, a web app where visitors collect QR codes from tourist spots to stamp their passport and compete on a ranking.
- For brainstorming, the team churned out a large number of ideas on Post-its and left the sorting and organizing entirely to AI.
  - The neighboring team fed a photo of their whiteboard straight into AI, skipping the hassle of typing everything in by hand.
- Kano-san told Bob, "Ask us if anything's unclear," which let it surface missing requirements on its own.
  - Bob asked questions about things like how to join the game, the authentication method, and the tech stack, and the spec was settled by having humans choose from its options.
- Tech stack: Node.js, SQLite, WebSocket, QR codes, containers (Docker)
- He specified Bootstrap CSS to auto-generate the website (only the images were made with another tool).
- Event attendees scanned the QR codes with their phones, and the stamp rally was demoed live in real time.
- Recommended development approach:
  - **Don't**: build something quick and improve it later (this leads to lots of bugs, growing complexity, and rework)
  - **Do**: nail down the requirements and spec first, then generate the app (fewer bugs, and it comes together all at once)

### Theme 3: Developing Izu Explorer (Kohei Nishikawa)

  - Tech stack: Node.js 24 (LTS), SQLite3, OpenStreetMap, Docker Compose

  - The plan file covered things like the API endpoints, screen layout, table design, and dependencies.
  - Because the spec was put together carefully, the resulting app worked with almost no errors.
- He set Bob running overnight and left it, then finished up by debugging the next morning — an efficient workflow.
- User state was handled with a combination of session IDs and SQLite (a full login feature was skipped due to time constraints).
- The souvenir and food-walk recommendation features were built on the OpenAI (GPT-4o) API — in hindsight, he felt he should have used WatsonX instead.
- He set things up so Docker Compose could bring up all the services at once.

## Ichiro Kano: Developing the Izu Stamp Rally App and Lessons in Using AI

- Ichiro Kano works on server and global manufacturing technology and cloud management services, and on the side also runs an IT services business and teaches programming to kids.
- At the hackathon, Kano-san's team of six — mostly engineers — had a lively brainstorm around the "money-making app" theme, generating plenty of ideas, but they couldn't keep up with sorting them all within the time limit.
- Turning that time crunch to their advantage, the team fed the Post-it notes from the brainstorm straight into IBM Bob, letting AI take over the sorting and grouping step entirely.
- Before starting development, they told Bob, "Ask us if anything's unclear," and settled the spec — the game-joining method, user authentication, and the tech stack (Node.js, SQLite, WebSocket, Docker) — through that back-and-forth.

- In the talk, Kano-san ran a live demo where attendees scanned QR codes to collect tourist-spot stamps and tried out the ranking and next-destination voting features firsthand.
- He stressed how important it is to build a solid spec before developing the app, concluding that doing so sharply cuts down on later changes and fixes and lets you build a polished, low-bug app all at once.

## Ichiro Kano: Introducing the "AI Employee" App (an Automated Crypto Trading System)

- Seeing OpenAI's API embedded inside Nishikawa-san's app at the hackathon inspired Kano-san to build his own app with AI built in, which he introduced here.
- He built an automated crypto trading app that runs unattended, 24/7, naming it "AI Employee." In version 1.0, he manually tuned the logic himself (grid trading, and detecting up/down trends).
- He ran it with 100,000 yen in seed capital over 3 months of automated trading, netting only a few yen of profit per trade — and then a major crypto crash left him with losses more than 10 times the profit he'd made.
- Drawing on what he learned at the hackathon, he evolved it into version 2.0 over summer break, building in OpenAI (GPT-5.6 Luna) so the AI itself analyzes the trade history and rewrites its own parameters autonomously.
- It's designed to feed in prompts every 1, 3, 6, and 24 hours to review past trades, continuously optimizing the parameters while limiting how often it calls the API to keep costs down.
- It integrates with two external APIs — the exchange and OpenAI — logging trade history in JSON so the AI keeps learning from past trends, giving it a self-evolving, agent-like architecture.
- Even though it's still running at a loss, he stressed his amazement that we've reached an era where an AI-embedded app can be built in about two days, and encouraged attendees to build an app they can seriously use for a hobby they love.
